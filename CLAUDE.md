## Channel v5 direction (Trường, 2026-11-25: "tự đánh giá kênh và cải thiện chất lượng nội dung, có thể xây thêm chủ đề khác để tăng tính thu hút, làm tốt hơn")

Self-assessment that drives this (numbers in `research/shorts-results.md`, 2026-11-18 block): accurate and on-brand but not gripping. Avg view ~9 s, stayed 42 % (target ≥ 60 %), 15.2 K views / 28 days, +8 subscribers (almost no reason to follow). Frames look like slides: same navy background in every video, objects small in a mostly empty frame, mascot small and static; flat TTS voice; almost no sound effects; 52 episodes in ~7 weeks with near-identical templates (also the profile YouTube's "inauthentic / template-based" policy describes, relevant if monetisation ever becomes a goal).

Content changes (apply from the next run; judge each by the run loop numbers):
1. **Topics: widen the hook, keep the promise "one everyday why, shown visibly".** Three new pillars, each a playlist when its first video ships: **Kitchen Science** (cooking and food you can try at home tonight), **Animals Explained** (why pets and familiar animals do odd things), **Why Is It Designed Like That?** (everyday objects and the reason behind their shape: round plane windows, QWERTY, yellow pencils, holes in pen caps). Weight new ideas toward these and "Your Brain Is Tricking You" / "Your Body, Explained", which hold people longest. Formats inside any pillar: try-it-now (DO), myth vs. fact shown by experiment, "weird result first". No flat claim openers.
2. **Story, not lecture.** Every script has a mini mystery: a weird result at 0 s, a wrong guess the viewer probably holds, the reveal at 7–10 s, one surprising consequence, loop. One person-sized stake per video ("this is why your ice cream bag works").
3. **Real object in the first 3 s, drawn.** Trường decided on 2026-11-26 not to film phone clips: everything stays drawn, so the drawn object must be recognisable and odd at thumbnail size in frame 0. Do not ask him for clips.
4. **Fill the frame and vary the look.** Subject ≥ 60 % of the frame, camera moves (push-in on the key detail, cut every ≤ 2.5 s), mascot large with a visible reaction at each beat, one accent palette per pillar instead of navy everywhere.
5. **Sound design layer.** Add short synthesized SFX on every visible event (pop, whoosh, tick, rising tone at the reveal); music still mix 15. Claude cannot hear: list the SFX timings in the report so Trường can check one video per run.
6. **Voice test.** Prepare 2–3 alternative narrations (other Kokoro voices with more pitch movement, or Trường's own recording) on one Short and let Trường choose before changing the default.
7. **Fewer, better.** 3 Shorts a week (one per pillar group) plus a long video only when its Short beat the channel median; no batch of 8 in one run.
8. **Community.** Every Short ends on a question viewers can answer in one word; pin it as a comment and reply to early comments in the next run.

## Shorts v4 playbook (Trường, 2026-11-18: "mọi người xem rất ít, một phần do chất lượng video và kịch bản"; he asked for every run to learn and improve, and for Claude to study more engaging scripts and topics)

Evidence (full table in `research/shorts-results.md`): 28-day views 15.2 K, +8 subscribers. The best Shorts (eyes 1.3 K, wheels, fingers, toast) hold people about 10–14 s; the worst hold 4–6 s; the length (21–55 s) makes no difference, and the 21–27 s Shorts of eps 31–40 did not do better than the 40–50 s ones. Openers that were a flat claim ("Metal isn't colder than wood", "Every banana…") or an abstract question did worst; openers where the viewer does something or sees a weird result did best. Scripts were explain-first and nothing changes at 7–10 s, so people leave there.

Rules from now on (Shorts; a change needs numbers, see the run loop):
1. **Hook first.** Frame one is already moving on a real object, with 3–6 words of text. Never open with a spoken claim sentence. Tag every Short with one hook type from `research/hook-swipe-file.md` (DO, WEIRD, STAKE, GUESS, COUNTDOWN).
2. **Write three hook lines per idea, pick one.** Before the full render, render the first 3 s alone, look at frame one at thumbnail size and at 1 s and 3 s; if a stranger would not stop, redo it.
3. **Beat the 10-second wall.** A second visible event (a reveal, a change of scene, the answer to the guess) at 7–10 s. The explanation comes after the loop is open, not before.
4. **One idea, 20–30 s, no recap sentence.** The last frame loops into the first. Do not pad to a length; do not cut a payoff to hit a length.
5. **Fewer, better.** 2–3 strong Shorts a run beat 8–10 thin ones; a run may stop at one if the hook gate fails. A long video only when a Short has shown the topic works.
6. **Choose topics by score** (a–e in the swipe file, build at 8 of 10) and prefer ideas the viewer can try in 10 s. Never choose for search volume or monetisation (Trường's 2026-10-03 rule stands).
7. **Do not oversell the hook.** The payoff must be seen in the video; an unpaid tease costs more than a plain opener.

Run loop (every Wednesday and Friday run; replaces the idea and script gates, in line with the 2026-11-13 creative-control decision):
- **L0 Measure.** Read every Short's views, stayed %, average view duration (method: "Lessons from the 2026-11-18 Claude in Chrome session"). Append a block to `research/shorts-results.md`. No browser reachable: say so and use the last block.
- **L1 Score last run's predictions** in the Hypotheses table (supported, rejected, too early with the days online) and write the one lesson with its evidence in Learnings. A Short under 48 h online is too early.
- **L2 Study (about 5 searches, not more).** Look for one new thing about hooks, topics or retention for explainer or science Shorts: web search plus, when a browser is linked, YouTube search results for the five pillars (titles, views and age only; never download or copy). Add what you learned as a dated row in the swipe file with the source, labelled as a hypothesis. Add 2–3 scored topic candidates to its candidate table.
- **L3 Make.** Pick ideas from the candidates and the backlog by score; build as above; fact-check with a web-search subagent before voicing (still required).
- **L4 Predict.** Put a prediction (stayed %, average view seconds) for each new Short in the Hypotheses table before upload.
- **L5 Report to Trường in Vietnamese, short:** what the numbers said, what changed because of it, what was made and the predictions, and what could not be checked (audio, Studio not reachable).
- Success target for a Short (third-party benchmarks, directional): stayed ≥ 60 %, average view ≥ 60 % of its length. Against our own: beat the channel median (about 9 s average view) before judging anything else.
- Keep CLAUDE.md small: Learnings under about 60 lines, data goes to `research/`, merge or drop stale lessons.

## Lessons from the 2026-11-26 agent batch (eps 55–60; three build agents, two episodes each)

- Setup that worked: one brief file (`agents/brief-v5-batch-55-60.md`) with the topic table, hard v5 rules and a shared `flock /tmp/wid-heavy.lock` around TTS, snapshot, render, encode and QC on the 2-core machine; six Shorts in ~40 min wall. Each agent wrote `epNN/retro.md` and read the others' before its second episode; second episodes needed fewer fix rounds.
- Recurring bugs (now checks before the first snapshot): two labels crossfading in one slot (hit 4 of 6) → write a label timetable per scene, first out before second in; mascot pop-mark zone (x 220–360, y 950–1080) and kara box (x 330–970, y 1352–1500) kept clear in layout constants; a scene can pass "change every 2.5 s" at scene starts and still sit still inside 7–10 s → check every 2.5 s window of the guess scene; time each visual event to the spoken word (char share of the beat), not round numbers; grep for 1e9 sentinels; run `npx hyperframes check` first (a helper named `clock` collided with build.js and rendered empty canvases).
- Hook gate changed two hooks: glow must be the brightest, most saturated thing at thumbnail size (ep 55); a wrong guess must be one viewers actually hold ("not for the ink", not "factory mistake").
- ASR rewrites: "Lay a wooden" → "leia wouldn't", "pasta starch" → "postastarch", "tick" → "take"; keep acronyms (ISO) on screen, not in narration.
- Still weak in all six: mid-video diagram scenes on dark palettes (lit 12–22 %); Trường should listen to one (SFX levels, British voices).
- Cold review round (independent agent, contact sheets only, `agents/review-55-60.md`) scored the six 5–6/10 although the builders' own critic passes had passed them. Patterns: 5 of 6 cut from the real object to a small dark diagram at 7–8 s (exactly where viewers leave); frame 0 showed the weird result in only 1 of 6; labels 22–28 px and hairline arrows; 2.5–4.5 s stretches where only text changes; same template look in all six. **Standing gate from ep 61: every Short gets an independent cold review before upload, and must pass:** frame 0 at 120 px wide shows the object and what is odd, and the 0–3 s narration matches the screen; the hook object stays on screen 7–15 s (explain by pushing into it or cutting it open, not by cutting to a diagram); something moves on the object in every 2 s window of 0–15 s; text ≥ 40 px, lines ≥ 8 px; captions on screen by the first syllable; mascot and pop marks off the subject; the ending question is about the topic; for try-it illusions the eye jump spans > half the screen, then ≥ 3 clean seconds.
- Round 2 (each builder fixed the review, then a second fresh reviewer, `agents/review2-55-60.md`): 5–6 → 6–8/10, no P0 left; 55, 56, 59, 60 upload-ready, 57 and 58 sent to round 3. What lifted scores most: keeping the hook object on screen and explaining inside it (cut the eye open, zoom into the palate). Three rules added: (1) pop marks live in a fixed slot beside the mascot and a layout check fails if they touch the subject (the most persistent defect across rounds); (2) when the voice says look / watch / see, that thing fills ≥ 60 % of the frame within 0.3 s; (3) try-it tests get a clean stage: eye jump ≥ 1,000 px, title changes with the instruction, 3 s with only the tested thing moving. Labels never repeat the caption; numbers ≥ 44 px. A builder's self-score runs ~1 point above the independent one: trust the independent score.
- Round 3 (third fresh reviewer, `agents/review3-55-60.md`): all six 7–7.5/10, no P0, all upload-ready. Scores by round (independent): 55 6→8→7.5 · 56 6→7→7.5 · 57 5→6→7 · 58 6→6→7 · 59 6→8→7.5 · 60 5→7.5→7.5. Fix rounds inside the drawn template top out around 7.5–8: same title box, caption box, small static mascot, dark gradient and flat shapes in every video, and no real footage. **Next batch (ep 61+) must:** (1) since Trường will not film clips (2026-11-26), make the drawn object in frame 0 recognisable and odd at thumbnail size; (2) one text layer: captions carry the sentence, labels are 1–2-word part names, numbers or a struck-out guess, and the build fails when a label shares ≥ 3 words with the caption on screen; (3) explain inside the filled, moving object (≥ 60 % of the frame even for clocks, charts, photos), checked by a pixel-change test on every 2 s window, with the strongest visual event (burst, blackout, flip) at 7.5–9 s; (4) vary the layout between videos (title/caption position, mascot size and role).

**Title** (46 chars): Why Do Water Drops Dance on a Hot Pan? #Shorts

**Description**

```
A hotter pan makes a water drop last longer. Past about 200 °C (it depends on the pan), the drop's underside boils instantly and it floats on its own steam: the Leidenfrost effect.

Sources
- Quéré (2013), Leidenfrost dynamics, Annual Review of Fluid Mechanics 45, 197–215
- Biance, Clanet & Quéré (2003), Leidenfrost drops, Physics of Fluids 15, 1632
- Bernardin & Mudawar (1999), The Leidenfrost point: experimental study and assessment of existing models, Journal of Heat Transfer 121, 894–903
- Burton et al. (2012), Geometry of the vapor layer under a Leidenfrost drop, Physical Review Letters 109, 074301
- Harvey, Mendez Harper & Burton (2021), Physical Review Letters (minimum Leidenfrost temperature)
```

# Ep07 Finger wrinkling: skeptic review

Reviewed 2026-11-02 against claims.md and script.json beats.

## Errors

1. **"Your arms don't do it. Your back doesn't do it. Only your fingers and your toes."**
   This is wrong as stated. Water-induced wrinkling happens on glabrous skin generally: fingers, toes, **palms and soles** (claims row 1). The script itself later says palms and soles have "the hardware this reflex needs", which contradicts "only".
   Severity: **error**.
   Suggested: "Your arms don't do it. Your back doesn't do it. Mainly your fingers and toes, and a little on palms and soles."

## Misleading

2. **"By the end of this video, you will know why your brain's wiring decides whether you wrinkle..."**
   The evidence (nerve lesions, replanted fingers, sympathetic function tests) points to the peripheral sympathetic nerves. Nothing in the claims shows the brain decides anything. Whether a central reflex is involved isn't established.
   Suggested: "...you will know why your nerves decide whether you wrinkle, and why scientists still argue about what it is for."

3. **"That is exactly what a soaked outer layer would do, glued to the tissue beneath it."**
   This overclaims, and the script's own source contradicts it. Sáez & Zöllner (claims row 3) found swelling alone needs more than about 10 % volume increase to wrinkle, more than is seen in real skin. So in practice a soaked layer alone would *not* do this.
   Suggested: "That is roughly what a soaked outer layer would try to do, glued to the tissue beneath it."

4. **"If swelling were the whole answer, though, every bit of skin that soaks should wrinkle the same way. It doesn't."**
   The logic is weak. Glabrous skin has a much thicker stratum corneum than arm or back skin, so a pure swelling model *also* predicts that fingers wrinkle more. A sharp viewer will spot this. The decisive evidence is the nerve-lesion result, so lean on that.
   Suggested: "But swelling can't be the whole answer, and the proof came from an unexpected place."

5. **"It takes several minutes in warm water, which is about how long this chain of signals needs to build up."**
   This causal explanation for the time course is unsupported. Nothing in the claims attributes the minutes-long delay to "signals building up". Water diffusing into the stratum corneum and sweat ducts is at least as plausible a rate-limiting step.
   Suggested: "None of this is instant. It takes several minutes in warm water before the wrinkles show."

## Caveats

6. **"Your fingertips are covered in sweat pores. Water seeps in through them, into the skin."**
   This step belongs to the hypothesised chain (claims row 7, "probably"), but it is stated flatly. The "seems" hedge three sentences earlier fades by this point.
   Suggested: "Your fingertips are covered in sweat pores. Water likely seeps in through them, into the skin."

7. **"Computer models of fingertip mechanics found that either effect alone needs a lot to make wrinkles."**
   This is one modelling study (Sáez & Zöllner 2017), not a body of "computer models".
   Suggested: "One computer model of fingertip mechanics found that either effect alone needs a lot to make wrinkles."

8. **"That is the hardware this reflex needs."**
   This is inference, not established fact. It is plausible, but no source shows that sweat-pore density is *required*.
   Suggested: "That may be the hardware this reflex needs."

9. **"...That suggests the blood vessels underneath set the map."**
   The hedge is fine, and the authors (German et al. 2025) do make this interpretation. But the claims table (row 11) records only the repeat finding, so add the blood-vessel interpretation to the table with its source.

10. **"...the blood flow returns, the pad fills back up, and within about half an hour the wrinkles are gone."**
    The resolution mechanism is presented as fact. The stratum corneum also dries and deswells. The "half an hour" figure is fine.
    Suggested: "...the skin dries, blood flow returns, the pad fills back up, and within about half an hour the wrinkles are gone."

11. **"In 2013, a team at Newcastle University tested it. ... With wrinkled fingers, they were faster."** / **"...a team in Berlin ran a similar test with forty people..."**
    Giving the sample size for the null study only (40) and not for the positive one (20) tilts the comparison. Also, Newcastle found no difference for *dry* objects, which is the key control.
    Suggested: "...a team at Newcastle University tested it with twenty people. With wrinkled fingers, they moved wet marbles faster, but not dry ones."

12. **"If wrinkling is controlled by nerves, it might be there for a reason."**
    This is a non sequitur. Nerve control doesn't imply an adaptive function. It's hedged ("might"), so this is a caveat only.

13. **"The how is fairly clear."**
    The trigger step (electrolyte change) is still "probably" (claims row 7). "The effector side is fairly clear" would be more accurate. Acceptable as is if the "probably" stays in the mechanism scene.

14. **"But the real trigger is your nervous system."**
    Strictly, water is the trigger and the nervous system is what carries out the response.
    Suggested: "But the real driver is your nervous system."

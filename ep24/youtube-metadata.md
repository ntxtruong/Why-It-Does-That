**Title** (54 chars): Why Do Soda Bubbles Rise From the Same Spots? #science

**Description**

```
Each string of bubbles in a glass of soda usually starts at a tiny hollow fibre, like lint from a towel, stuck to the glass. A brand-new bubble is so small that surface tension squeezes its gas back into the drink, so bubbles need a pocket of gas that is already there. The fibre traps one, fills with carbon dioxide from the drink, and releases a bubble again and again. (Most of this research was done on champagne; the same physics applies to soda.)
```

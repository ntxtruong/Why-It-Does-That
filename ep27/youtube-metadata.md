**Description**

Mint isn't cold: it rings your cold alarm. Nerves in your mouth carry a tiny cold sensor (the ion channel TRPM8), a gate that opens when the temperature drops. Menthol, the molecule in mint, latches onto that same gate and makes it open at warmer temperatures, even in a warm mouth. The nerve fires and your brain reads: cold. That's why a sip of water after a mint can feel icy. Chili does the same trick on your burning-heat sensor (TRPV1).

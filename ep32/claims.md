# Ep 32 claims table

| Claim | Value | Source | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A self-produced touch feels less tickly than the same touch from outside ("can't really tickle yourself": weaker, not zero) | rated significantly less tickly, intense and pleasant | Weiskrantz, Elliott & Darlington (1971) Nature 230: 598–599; Blakemore, Wolpert & Frith (1998) Nature Neuroscience 1: 635–640 | high |
| The brain predicts the sensory result of your own movement and attenuates it | forward-model prediction (efference copy) | Blakemore, Wolpert & Frith (2000) NeuroReport 11: R11–R16 "Why can't you tickle yourself?" | high |
| In an experiment people moved one hand to stroke their own other palm through a robot (foam on the right palm) | robot interface | Blakemore, Frith & Wolpert (1999) J Cogn Neurosci, "Spatio-temporal prediction modulates the perception of self-produced stimuli" (as summarised in Blakemore, Wolpert & Frith 1999, NeuroImage 10: 448–459) | high |
| Adding a delay made the touch feel more tickly | delays 0, 100, 200, 300 ms; progressive increase 0 → 200 ms | same | high for 200 ms = "a fifth of a second" and "tickled more" (not "as much as someone else") |

Misconception avoided: not claimed that self-tickling is impossible ("really"), nor that a delay makes it equal to an external tickle.

# Ep 55 claims (cats' eyes glow)
| Claim | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Cat eyes do not make light; the glow is your light reflected back (eyeshine) | Wikipedia "Tapetum lucidum" (retroreflector); sci.news 2022 |
| The tapetum lucidum is a reflective layer directly behind the retina | Wikipedia "Tapetum lucidum"; Wikipedia "Cat vision" |
| Light that passes the photoreceptors is reflected back through them (a second chance to be absorbed) | Wikipedia (reflects light back through the retina, more light for the photoreceptors); sci.news ("gives the retina a second chance to absorb more light") |
| Some reflected light leaves the eye back toward the light source = the glow | Wikipedia (retroreflector, eyeshine) |
| Humans have no tapetum (our red-eye in photos is a weak reflection from the blood-rich choroid) | Wikipedia "Tapetum lucidum"; sci.news (primates incl. humans lack one) |
| Cats see with about one sixth of the light humans need | Wikipedia "Cat vision" ("roughly one-sixth the amount of light", cited) |
| Price: light scatter slightly lowers sharpness | Wikipedia ("slightly compromises visual acuity"); sci.news ("a little fuzzier") |
| Cat eyeshine is often yellow-green to yellow-orange (varies) | sci.news; Wikipedia (light orange to green in adults) |
Notes: pictures are schematic and not to scale; the photon dots show where light goes, not counts. The real tapetum lies in the upper back of the eye; the drawing shows it as a band on the back wall. "Yours don't" is about eyeshine; human red-eye in flash photos is a different, much weaker reflection (mentioned in the description).

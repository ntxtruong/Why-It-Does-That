# Ep 57 claims (wooden spoon stops a pot boiling over)
| Claim | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Plain boiling water does not climb the pot; starch (pasta, rice, potato) and milk protein make the bubbles sturdy so they stack up and climb out | The Conversation / ScienceAlert, Deagon & Mann, "A Simple Science Based Trick Really Can Delay Your Pot From Boiling Over" (7 May 2025); CQUniversity news (same authors); Mental Floss quoting Gizmodo ("starch molecules ... make the bubbles extra sturdy") |
| A wooden spoon laid across the pot bursts bubbles that touch it: it lowers the surface temperature and is porous (dry wood more than wet) | The Conversation / ScienceAlert ("lowers the surface temperature and provides a porous surface to burst the bubbles"); Mental Floss quoting Michelle Dickinson ("colder and dryer wood ... the bubble bursts") |
| Wood conducts heat poorly, so it stays cooler than the foam | Mental Floss (Dickinson) |
| One burst makes touching bubbles burst too, so the foam does not build up | Mental Floss ("the bubbles touching them tend to burst too") |
| It only buys time: once the spoon is as hot as the liquid and water-logged it stops working | The Conversation / ScienceAlert ("will soon become the same temperature as the liquid"); Mental Floss ("so warm and water-logged") |
| NOT claimed: "wood is hydrophobic" (no source supports it); no times or temperatures are stated | — |

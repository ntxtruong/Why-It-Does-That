# Ep 59 claims (falling cat righting reflex)
| Claim | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Marey's chronophotographs of a falling cat, 1894 (Comptes Rendus, La Nature); the cat started with no rotation | Wikipedia "Falling cat problem" |
| Physicists at the time suspected the cat "cheated" (used the handler's hand) because a rigid body cannot do this | Wikipedia "Falling cat problem" |
| The cat is not rigid: it bends at the middle so front and back halves rotate about different axes (Kane & Scher 1969 two-cylinder model) | Wikipedia "Falling cat problem"; "Cat righting reflex" |
| Front legs tucked: front half turns up to ~90° while the stretched back half turns the opposite way by as little as ~10°; then the legs swap | Wikipedia "Cat righting reflex" |
| Works within conservation of angular momentum ("nothing to push on" = no external torque) | Wikipedia "Cat righting reflex"; Live Science (Gbur, Falling Felines and Fundamental Physics, 2019) |
| From less than about 0.3–0.6 m cats are unlikely to turn properly (no time) | Live Science, citing Annals of Improbable Research |
| Reflex appears at 3–4 weeks, perfected at 6–9 weeks; vestibular system (inner ear) tells which way is down | Wikipedia "Cat righting reflex"; Live Science |
Notes: the picture shows the roll of each half as a cylinder seen from the side, schematic and not to scale; turning angles are illustrative. The video shows no person dropping a cat; the 1894 frames are drawn, not copied from Marey.

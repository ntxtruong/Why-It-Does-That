# Ep 60 claims (brain freeze)
| Claim | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Cold food/drink drops the temperature of the palate; its blood vessels tighten, then reopen (rebound dilation) | Johns Hopkins Medicine, "How to Ease Brain Freeze"; Wikipedia "Cold-stimulus headache" (constriction then rebound vasodilation, "thought to be") |
| The pain signal travels on the trigeminal nerve, whose upper branch reaches the forehead, so the pain is felt there (referred pain) | Johns Hopkins Medicine; Wikipedia |
| Your brain is not freezing (the pain starts in the palate) | Johns Hopkins; Cleveland Clinic (pain from vessel changes, felt in the front of the head) |
| Vessel widening measured: anterior cerebral artery flow rose and the artery dilated during brain freeze, 13 volunteers (shown only as "flood open"; artery not named on screen) | Serrador et al., Experimental Biology 2012 (Live Science, ScienceDaily) |
| One trial: eating 100 ml of ice cream in < 5 s gave 27 % (20/73) headaches vs 13 % (9/72) when eaten over > 30 s; RR 2.2, 145 pupils | Kaczorowski & Kaczorowski, BMJ 2002;325:1445 (PMC139031) |
| Press the tongue (or thumb) to the roof of the mouth to warm it; warm water helps | Johns Hopkins Medicine; Cleveland Clinic. No trial: narration says "Doctors suggest" |
| Lasts seconds to about two minutes (description only) | Cleveland Clinic |
